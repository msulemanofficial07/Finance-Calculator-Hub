What is a systematic investment plan?

A systematic investment plan (SIP) is a way to invest a set amount at regular intervals, often monthly. Rather than choosing one large contribution date, an investor makes a series of smaller contributions. This calculator models that schedule; it does not recommend a particular investment or fund.

How it works

Each month, the calculator treats the entered contribution as a new payment, applies the monthly rate implied by the annual assumption and compounds it through the remaining period. The result combines all payments and projected growth. An optional inflation setting can estimate the same amount in today's purchasing power.

How to use this SIP calculator

  1. Enter a monthly contribution. Use an amount that is realistic and repeatable.
  2. Choose an annual return assumption. This is an editable scenario, not a forecast or guarantee.
  3. Set the investment period. Years are converted into 12 monthly contribution periods.
  4. Choose whether to adjust for inflation. This is set to No by default. Choose Yes to reveal the annual inflation field, then enter your own assumption.
  5. Review the estimate and yearly schedule. Compare contributions, growth and—if enabled—inflation-adjusted value.

Formula and assumptions

The model applies the annual rate divided by 12, then adds each monthly contribution at the end of the month. For a positive monthly rate, the resulting future value is P × [((1 + r)n − 1) / r], where P is the contribution, r is the monthly rate and n is the number of contributions. At a zero rate, the result is simply P × n. Contributions are assumed to remain constant; fees, taxes and changing returns are excluded.

If inflation is entered, purchasing power is estimated as Nominal future value ÷ (1 + i)t, where i is the inflation rate as a decimal and t is the number of years.

Interpreting a projection responsibly

Investment values can rise or fall, and actual returns may differ substantially from a smooth projection. Try more than one return assumption to see how sensitive the estimate is. Consider your goals, time horizon, costs, risk tolerance and local rules; speak with a qualified professional if you need personal advice.

Practical example

Suppose you contribute 500 each month for 10 years and enter a hypothetical 7% annual return. You would contribute 60,000 in total. After applying the month-end, monthly-compounding scenario, the calculator illustrates the difference between contributions and projected growth. Try 3% and 10% in the calculator to see that a smooth projection is sensitive to the assumed rate.

Understanding the result

The future-value amount is a scenario, not the value you should expect to receive. “Estimated growth” shows the projected amount above your contributions. If the result falls after changing an input, verify the entries and remember that negative or reduced returns may be possible in real markets.

Factors to consider

  • The tool assumes equal monthly contributions and a constant annual rate.
  • It does not include fund fees, platform charges, taxes or irregular withdrawals; inflation adjustment is optional.
  • Real investment returns can vary, and markets can produce losses.
  • Currency formatting does not account for exchange-rate movements.

SIP versus lump-sum investing

A SIP distributes purchases over a schedule, while lump-sum investing places a larger amount into an investment at once. A regular contribution can help make savings more consistent, but it does not guarantee a better investment result. Compare the approaches using the lump-sum calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Are SIP returns guaranteed?

No. This calculator uses a constant rate you choose for illustration. Market returns vary and may be negative.

Does the estimate include fees or taxes?

No. Fees, taxes and other investment-specific charges are not included in this estimate.

Can I use this calculator outside India?

Yes. Select a display currency and enter values in that currency. The calculation is a general contribution-growth illustration, not country-specific investment or tax guidance.

What does the inflation-adjusted value show?

It converts the nominal future estimate into an approximate purchasing-power amount using the inflation assumption you enter. It does not use a live inflation index or predict future prices.

Why does the result differ from the amount I contributed?

The result includes contributions plus estimated compounding. At a negative or low assumed return, projected growth may be low or negative.
