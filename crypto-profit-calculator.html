What this crypto profit estimator calculates

The calculator estimates a single buy-and-sell trade from the values you enter. It multiplies the buy and sell prices by quantity, subtracts purchase cost and total fees from sale proceeds, and applies your optional tax-rate assumption only when the trade has a positive estimated profit. It does not connect to an exchange, fetch prices or store a portfolio.

How to use the calculator

  • Buy price per coin: your entered acquisition price for one unit.
  • Sell price per coin: your entered disposal price for one unit.
  • Quantity: units sold in this example.
  • Total fees: the combined fees for this trade, in the selected currency.
  • Estimated tax rate: an optional percentage for scenario analysis, not a tax rate recommendation.

Prices, fees and proceeds must use the same currency and relate to the same trade. The currency selector formats the result; it does not convert prices.

Profit formula and limitations

Estimated pre-tax profit is sale price × quantity − buy price × quantity − fees. The optional estimated tax is the positive profit multiplied by the rate you enter. Actual taxable gains can depend on transaction history, cost-basis method, holding period, allowable expenses, losses, reporting thresholds and local law. This simplified estimate does not account for those details, staking, mining, swaps, gifts or multiple lots.

Check the rules where you file

Tax treatment of digital assets differs across the United States, United Kingdom, Australia, Canada and other jurisdictions, and rules can change. Keep your own transaction records and consult the relevant tax authority or a qualified tax professional for your circumstances. Do not rely on this calculator to prepare a tax return or make a trading decision.

Frequently asked questions

Does the calculator use live crypto prices?

No. Enter your own buy and sell prices. The calculator does not use an exchange or price feed.

Does the result tell me the tax I owe?

No. The optional rate only creates a rough illustration. Actual tax treatment depends on your jurisdiction and full circumstances.

What if my trade has a loss?

The estimate subtracts your entered costs and fees from proceeds. The optional tax estimate is set to zero for a non-positive result; this does not assess whether a loss is deductible.
