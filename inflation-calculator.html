What inflation means for purchasing power

Inflation is a rise in prices over time. When prices rise, the same amount of money may buy fewer goods and services than before. The change in a broad price index is not the same as every household's personal cost of living, because spending patterns and local prices vary.

How to use this inflation calculator

Enter an amount or item price in your chosen display currency, an annual inflation assumption and a number of years. The result shows both the estimated future price of that item and how much buying power the unchanged amount would have in today's money. The currency control only changes number formatting; it does not convert between currencies or use a live inflation index.

Formula and assumptions

The estimated future cost is Future cost = Amount today × (1 + i)t. The buying power of the unchanged amount is Today's-money value = Amount today ÷ (1 + i)t, where i is the annual inflation rate as a decimal and t is the number of years. For instance, a 3% assumption uses 0.03 each year. The estimate assumes the same rate every year; it is not a forecast.

Future cost and purchasing power are different questions

Future cost estimates how much money may be needed to buy an item with today's price. Today's-money buying power estimates what an unchanged amount could buy after prices rise. The calculator shows both to make the distinction clear. Personal expenses can rise faster or slower than a general price index, so use appropriate data for your own planning.

Frequently asked questions

Is this based on live inflation data?

No. You enter a constant annual rate assumption. The tool does not fetch current or historical consumer-price data.

Why can my personal inflation differ?

Household costs vary with location and spending patterns. A general inflation assumption may not match the prices you personally experience.

Does the displayed currency change the calculation?

No. It changes the currency symbol and formatting only; the calculator does not perform exchange-rate conversions.
