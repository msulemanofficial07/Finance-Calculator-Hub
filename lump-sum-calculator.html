What is a lump-sum investment?

A lump-sum investment places a single amount into an investment at one time. This differs from regular contributions, where money is added periodically. This calculator models an initial amount left invested over a chosen number of years, with annual compounding at a constant rate entered by you.

How to use this calculator

Enter the amount invested today, an assumed annual return and the number of years. Select your preferred display currency; the estimate updates as you change the inputs. The currency selection only formats the result and does not exchange or convert the input.

Future value formula

For principal P, annual rate i as a decimal and t years, this calculator uses annual compounding: A = P × (1 + i)t. The estimated growth is the projected future amount minus your starting principal. This assumes no deposits or withdrawals after the initial investment, with no taxes, fees or inflation adjustments included.

Compare lump sum and regular contributions

A one-time investment and a monthly investment have different cash-flow timing. Outcomes depend on when each contribution is invested and what happens in markets along the way. To model recurring deposits, try the SIP calculator. Neither calculator forecasts returns or tells you which strategy is right for you; assess your own circumstances and risk tolerance.

Frequently asked questions

Does this calculator predict an investment return?

No. The annual rate is a user-entered assumption used for an illustration, not a forecast or guarantee.

Are fees and inflation included?

No. The result excludes fees, taxes and inflation. These factors can materially affect actual purchasing power and returns.

How is this different from the compound interest calculator?

Both model annual compounding for an initial balance. This page focuses on a one-time investment scenario; the compound interest page explains the underlying compounding calculation.
