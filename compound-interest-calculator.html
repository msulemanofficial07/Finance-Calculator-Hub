What is compound interest?

Compound interest is growth calculated on an initial balance and on growth already accumulated. If interest remains invested, later periods can earn returns on a larger balance. The effect depends on the rate, compounding frequency, time and any deposits or withdrawals. This tool illustrates a single starting balance with no additional contributions.

How to use the calculator

Enter an initial amount, an annual rate assumption and a time horizon. The calculator updates the estimate as you change the inputs and shows the starting principal alongside estimated interest. The currency selector changes how values are displayed; it does not convert an entered amount using live exchange rates.

Formula and assumptions

For an initial principal P, annual rate i expressed as a decimal, and t years with annual compounding, the calculation is A = P × (1 + i)t. For example, a 6% annual assumption applies a 1.06 factor once each year. The model assumes a fixed rate and annual compounding; it does not account for taxes, fees, variable rates, deposits, withdrawals or inflation.

Compare scenarios instead of relying on one rate

Future rates are uncertain. Compare lower and higher assumptions, and consider how inflation and fees could affect purchasing power. A savings account may use a different rate convention, and an investment is not the same as a guaranteed deposit. Check product terms and local protections before making financial decisions.

Frequently asked questions

Is the annual rate guaranteed?

No. It is an input you choose to model a hypothetical scenario; actual interest or investment returns can change.

How often does this calculator compound?

It compounds once per year using the annual rate you enter.

Does this include additional deposits?

No. This calculator models a one-time starting balance. Use the SIP calculator to estimate regular monthly contributions.
